# Issue 15 — Novelty Effects: Why Your Game Feature's First Week Is Lying to You

Companion code for *Analytics Playbook: Novelty Effects in Feature A/B Tests: Why Early Wins Mislead (Gaming Industry example)*. A fictional mobile studio, **Nova Peak Games**, A/B tests a new **Daily Spin Wheel** feature in a made-up game, **Dragon's Ascent**. The day-1 lift looks huge (28%) but is mostly a novelty effect — early curiosity, not the feature's true value. This repo shows two ways to find the real, steady-state effect: watching the lift decay over 30 days, and comparing new players (who never experienced a "before" version) against existing players.

## What the simulation models

Two player groups, each with a control and treatment arm:

- **Existing players** (4,000 per arm) — present for all 30 days. The Spin Wheel is genuinely *new* to them, so their treatment response includes a novelty bump on top of the true effect. The bump starts at +23 percentage points on day 1 and decays exponentially (time constant of 7 days) toward the steady-state lift of 5%.
- **New players** (120 per arm, per day) — join fresh each day, after the feature already exists. It's just part of the game to them, so there's no novelty to decay — their treatment response is the steady-state effect (5%) from day one.

```
true_lift(day) = STEADY_LIFT + NOVELTY_BUMP * exp(-(day - 1) / DECAY_TAU)
```

## Real output from this dataset

```
Daily lift, existing players:
day  1: 28.0%
day  3: 21.9%
day  7: 14.7%
day 14: 8.7%
day 21: 6.1%
day 30: 5.0%

Three ways to estimate the effect:
Existing players, days 1-3 (early peek)          lift= 25.2%  (needs ~3 days of data)
New players, any day (pooled)                    lift=  4.9%  (needs ~1 days of data)
Existing players, days 25-30 (waited it out)      lift=  5.4%  (needs ~30 days of data)
```

Both accurate estimates (new-player pooled and late existing-player) land within half a point of each other — the new-player read gets you there in 1 day instead of 30.

## Adapting this to real data

- Replace `generate_dummy_data.py` with a query that pulls daily average sessions (or your metric of choice) by day, arm, and whether the player joined before or after the feature launched.
- The "new player" cutoff should be the feature's launch date — anyone who signed up after that date has never seen the "before" state.
- If your test doesn't run long enough to see a late/steady-state window, the new-player comparison is your only fast, unbiased read — make sure you're routing new signups into the test.
- Watch for small new-player sample sizes early in the test; the new-player estimate gets noisy if you don't have enough daily signups per arm.
